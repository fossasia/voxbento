If your event streams on YouTube, open the room page, go to **Room audio** and choose
**From a YouTube stream**. VoxBento then adds live captions, translations and translated
speech. The guide asks two things and shows only the setup that fits.

## Step 1: Which broadcast?

**Sign in with YouTube** with the channel that streams the event and choose the room's
broadcast from the list (or paste its link). VoxBento can then:

- fill in the broadcast's YouTube stream key itself when it forwards your stream, so nobody
  copies keys out of YouTube Studio;
- show whether YouTube is receiving the stream;
- put one caption language into YouTube's own **CC** button (see below).

Not your channel, or the server has no Google sign-in set up? Skip to step 2: captions,
translations and speech still work on VoxBento's listener page.

## Step 2: How does your video reach YouTube?

| Your setup | Choose | What you do | If VoxBento goes down |
|---|---|---|---|
| StreamYard, Restream, Zoom or Teams live streaming, vMix, or OBS with a multi-output plugin | **My streaming tool can send to two places** | Keep YouTube as a destination and add VoxBento as a *Custom RTMP* destination with the server and stream key the room page creates. | YouTube keeps going. |
| OBS, vMix, Wirecast or a hardware encoder with one output | **One encoder, and VoxBento forwards to YouTube** | Point the encoder at VoxBento's server and stream key. VoxBento passes the stream to YouTube unchanged, using the stream key filled in from step 1 or pasted by hand. | **Your YouTube stream stops too.** Do a test run with an unlisted broadcast first. |
| YouTube Studio's webcam or phone streaming, or a stream someone else runs | **I can't change how it streams** | Play the stream in a Chrome or Edge tab, open the sender, choose **A browser tab** and share that tab with its audio. Keep both tabs open. | YouTube keeps playing; captions pause until the sender is started again. |

The first two routes use the same server, stream key and health panel as **From streaming
software**; the guide shows them for you. The stream key is shown once, when you create it.
A route the server operator has not switched on is shown greyed out with what to ask for.
Until you pick a route, the guide shows the one the room uses now.

The browser sender cannot start while the room still takes a stream from streaming software.
Choosing **I can't change how it streams** in that case also shows the streaming-software card:
stop that stream with **Switch to the online meeting**, then start the sender.

## Captions in YouTube's CC button

With a broadcast chosen in step 1, **Captions on YouTube** sends finished captions in one
language (the spoken language or one of the room's translation languages) to the
broadcast's caption track. Set it **before the broadcast goes live**: YouTube does not let a
live broadcast switch to posted captions, unless *POST captions to URL* was already chosen
for it in YouTube Studio. Ultra-low latency broadcasts cannot show captions. YouTube carries
one caption track; viewers can use YouTube's auto-translate on it.

## Share the listener page

Every language and the translated speech are on VoxBento's listener page. Put its link in
the broadcast description so viewers who need another language can follow along.

## Why VoxBento can't just take a YouTube link

YouTube's developer policies do not allow downloading a stream or separating its audio, and
automated pulling from YouTube is blocked. So the sound has to come to VoxBento from your
streaming tool or from a browser tab that is playing the stream. VoxBento only reads your
broadcasts' details and posts caption text; it never downloads your video.

For the operator setup of Google sign-in and the stream relay, see
`docs/youtube-connect.md` and [Program Ingest & Relay](./program-ingest.mdx).
